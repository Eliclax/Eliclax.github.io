There will be 2 hours of teaching per week over 40 weeks, and I expect students to spend about {{ page.workload }} per week reviewing the material and doing homework. The total time spent in this course should be around 220 hours over the year.  As the follow-up to the Olympiad 1 (O1) course, this course will continue to develop creative problem-solving skills and proof-writing skills.

## Syllabus

- **Problem-Solving Strategies (5 weeks):** data extraction when small cases; more induction, extremal principal, and visualisations; counterexamples and meta-proofs.
- **Combinatorics (8 weeks):** more double counting, bijections, invariants, monovariants, and graph theory; combinatorial geometry; colouring problems and game theory; consideration of combinatorial processes.
- **Algebra (8 weeks):** real and integer polynomials; Cauchy-Schwarz and Jensen's inequalities; smoothing; consideration of sequence problems; functional substitutions; injectivity and surjectivity; graphing functional equations; periodicity; fixed points; multiplicative functional equations.
- **Number Theory (8 weeks):** more divisibility and modular arithmetic problems, including modular inverses; using quadratic residues and Vieta jumping to solve Diophantine Equations; p-adic valuations; constructions in number theory, including via prime numbers, highly composite numbers, and Chinese remainder theorem; dealing with digits, and dealing with floor function.
- **Geometry (8 weeks):** dealing with equal angle conditions; homothethy (dilations) and spiral symmetries; tips on constructing points and lines; Ceva and Menalaus theorems; moving points around in Geometry problems; trig bashing.
- **Proofs (1 weeks):** getting partial marks

## Prerequisites
Before starting this course, students should be familiar with most of the concepts taught in the Olympiad 1 (O1) course. In particular, students should:
- Understand what a proof is and be able to write one with an appropriate level of detail.
- Use contradiction and induction effectively in proofs.
- Be able to use a variety of soft and hard techniques in problem-solving.
- Apply a range of theorems and techniques across algebra, combinatorics, geometry, and number theory to prove results, especially in the areas covered in the O1 course.
- Understand what graph theory and functional equations are, along with some techniques used to analyse them.
- Understand how to use basic techniques to solve inequalities.
